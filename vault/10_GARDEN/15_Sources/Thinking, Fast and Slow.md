# 📖 Thinking, Fast and Slow

**👤 Author:** [[Daniel Kahneman]]
 Full Notes:

---

### 💡 The Core Argument (Thesis)
> Human judgment is produced by two systems that do not share a workload evenly. System 1 is fast, automatic, associative and always running; System 2 is slow, effortful, serial and lazy. Because System 2 is metabolically expensive, it defers to System 1 far more than we believe, and the resulting errors are not random noise — they are *systematic*, predictable, and survive knowing about them. Kahneman's claim is not that people are stupid but that intuition is a well-adapted machine being asked questions it was never built to answer.

### 🧠 Concepts Extracted
* **[[System 1 vs System 2 (Dual-Process Theory)]]:** The organizing frame of the book, and the source of the vault's most-linked concept.
* **[[Heuristic]]:** Substitution — when a hard question arrives, System 1 quietly answers an easier one and reports the result with full confidence.
* **[[Cognitive Bias]]:** The predictable residue of that substitution. Anchoring, availability, representativeness.
* **[[Prospect Theory]]:** With [[Amos Tversky]] — the model that replaced expected utility. Losses are weighted roughly twice as heavily as equivalent gains.
* **[[Cognitive Ease]]:** Fluency read as truth. Things that are easy to process feel more likely to be correct.
* **[[Working Memory]]:** The bottleneck that makes System 2 expensive in the first place — and the reason effortful thought is rationed rather than free.
