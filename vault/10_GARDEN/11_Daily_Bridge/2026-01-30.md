**📝 BRIEF SUMMARY:**
>The short paper written by Rena explains how reframing events as challenges as opposed to threats changes the release patterns of important hormones that contribute to states of stress. The overall takeaway is that when we perceive a stressful situation as a challenge that we are more apt to handle it in a way that is manageable and with higher levels of performance as a result of our hormone balances being more optimal for performance. She appeals to some work by [[Robert Sapolsky]] which indicates that contextual factors such as levels of happiness, self esteem, and social support explain variance in the way that individuals are able to cope with stress. This is important because it implies that those who live a life characterized by resilience, support, mental well being, etc... are more apt to deal with stress. Those who are already depressed and anxious are less prepared to deal with stress which emphasizes the importance of prioritizing our well being through lifestyle design. This is where philosophy comes in and I personally love to embody the values of [[Stoicism]] and [[Marcus Aurelius]], even if it is cliché to do so. Here is a quick and simple way of summing this up in terms of lifestyle design and self concept. I ought to project out into the future to first imagine the kind of lifestyle I want to lead. I will then reverse engineer that lifestyle to understand the necessary actions to pursue in order to manifest this lifestyle. I then must consider that actions follow identity or self concept. I must perform actions that align with a desirable self concept or identity "I am the type of person who makes wise decisions, I prioritize long term success > short term gains, and I consider how my actions affect my community". These internalizations reinforce behaviors that align with this identity, and vice versa. I allow my vision of my future to be informed by who I am, and I allow my visions of who I could be inform what I should do.
