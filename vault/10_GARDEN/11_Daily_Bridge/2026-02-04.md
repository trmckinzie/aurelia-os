**📝 BRIEF SUMMARY:**
>Modern humans can investigate the mind with many approaches. The subfields of psychology are important in contributing to the broader understanding of the scientific and philosophical understanding of the [[Mind]] and [[Behavior]]. [[Evolutionary Psychology]] unifies these fields with an empirical approach to the ontological and epistemological study of mind. I find it crucially important to take the theory of evolution by natural selection seriously, even if one's initial contact with the idea is purely in terms of biology. The reason for this is because as Dennett makes clear in [[Darwin's Dangerous Idea]], this idea is acidic and an internalization of the concept will slowly erode your very natural, intuitive, and anthropomorphic view of the world, which includes your role as an individual amongst the cosmos. Our evolved minds are still a great mystery, even to Evolutionary Psychologists. An acceptance of this worldview does initially shatter many assumptions about life, meaning, and morality, that you likely hold near and dear to your human heart, but the reassembly of this reality through the lens of an evolutionary naturalist, can be a process characterized by fulfillment, insight, creativity, and spirituality if you are open to it. And don't worry, this process of picking up the broken pieces of a broken worldview still comes with struggle, strife, and a fair amount of inevitable human suffering. This indicates that the deal is not "too good to be true". So you decide if you can stomach the red pill on your evolved mind or if you would prefer to appeal to intuition, folklore, and superstition. If you are up for the challenge, welcome to your evolved mind.
