# ⚛️ Computational Theory of Mind

**🔗 Related:** [[Turing Machine]], [[Language of Thought (Mentalese)]], [[Multiple Realizability]], [[The Chinese Room Argument]], [[Connectionism]], [[Information Theory]], [[Dualism vs. Monism]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The philosophical view that the human mind is literally an information processing system, and that cognition (thinking) is a form of computation.

- **The Ultimate Metaphor:** The Brain is the **Hardware** (the wet, biological circuitry). The Mind is the **Software** (the algorithms running on that circuitry).
    
- **The Mechanism:** Computation involves manipulating mental representations (symbols) according to strict, formal rules (syntax). When you "think," your brain is executing a biological algorithm.

### 📝 Key Insight
- Killing the "Ghost in the Machine":** Historically, CTM was revolutionary because it finally solved Descartes' Mind-Body problem. For centuries, philosophers couldn't explain how an immaterial "thought" could move a physical "arm" without relying on magic or a soul. CTM provided the answer: The mind isn't a ghost; it's a program. It made the mind safe for rigorous, materialistic science.

### 🏛️ The Three Pillars of the Theory

To understand CTM at a deep level, you must understand the three concepts that hold it up:

1. **Representationalism (The Data):** The mind interacts with the world by creating internal symbols that _stand in_ for external reality. When you think about a "dog," there is no physical dog in your head; there is a mental symbol representing the dog.
    
2. **Formal Rule Application (The Syntax):** The brain processes these symbols based solely on their physical "shape" (syntax), not what they mean (semantics). Just like a calculator manipulates the symbols "2", "+", and "2" to output "4" without "understanding" what a number is, the brain manipulates mental symbols to produce logical thoughts.
    
3. **Multiple Realizability (The Independence):** If the mind is just software, it is independent of its physical substrate. A program can run on a Mac, a PC, or a smartphone. Therefore, a "Mind" could theoretically run on biological neurons (a human), silicon chips (an AI), or even a vast system of gears and pulleys, as long as the computational architecture is correct.
    

### 🗺️ The Path to Deep Mastery (Concepts to Explore)

To truly grasp CTM, you need to explore the specific theories that built it and the arguments that try to tear it down:

- **[[The Turing Machine]] (Alan Turing):** You cannot understand CTM without understanding Turing. He proved mathematically that a machine reading a tape of 1s and 0s can compute _any_ computable logic. CTM proposes that the brain is essentially a biological, highly complex Turing Machine.
    
- **[[The Language of Thought]] / "Mentalese" (Jerry Fodor):** If the brain computes, what language is it programmed in? It isn't English or Spanish. Fodor argued we are born with an innate, subconscious programming language (Mentalese) consisting of primitive symbols and syntactic rules. We translate the world into Mentalese, compute the answer, and translate it back out as spoken language or action.
    
- **[[The Chinese Room Argument]] (John Searle):** This is the most famous weapon _against_ CTM. Searle imagines a man in a room manipulating Chinese symbols using a rulebook. To an outside observer, the man speaks fluent Chinese. But the man actually understands _zero_ Chinese; he is just following syntax rules. Searle argues this proves computation (syntax) can never produce true understanding or consciousness (semantics).
