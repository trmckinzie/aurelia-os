# ⚛️ Dendrite

**🔗 Related:** [[Neuron]], [[Synapse]], [[Axon]], [[Neuroplasticity]], [[Summation]], [[Action Potential]], [[Dendritic Spines]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> Branched, protoplasmic extensions of a nerve cell that propagate the electrochemical stimulation received from other neural cells to the cell body (Soma).

- **The Name:** Derived from the Greek _dendron_, meaning **"tree."** This is due to their highly branched, fractal-like structure.
    
- **The Function:** They act as the "Input" ports of the neuron. While a neuron typically has only one axon (output), it can have thousands of dendrites, allowing it to receive signals from up to 10,000 other neurons simultaneously.

### 📝 Key Insight
* **Neuroplasticity in Action:** Dendrites are the most dynamic part of the neuron. They can grow, shrink, and re-route in hours or days. This is the physical manifestation of your **[[Working Memory]]** becoming **[[Long-Term Memory]]**.
    
- **Information Density:** The "Dendritic Tree" is the reason the brain is so powerful. Because each dendrite can branch extensively, a single neuron can participate in thousands of different "conversations" at once, creating the massive parallel processing power seen in human cognition.

### 🏗️ Anatomy & The Logic of Input

- **Dendritic Spines:** These are tiny, mushroom-shaped protrusions on the surface of the dendrites. Each spine is a dedicated docking station for a single synapse.
    
    - _The Hardware of Learning:_ When you learn a new skill, your brain doesn't necessarily grow new neurons; it grows new **Dendritic Spines** to create new connections.
        
- **Synaptic Summation (The Calculator):** The dendrite isn't just a passive wire; it's a computer.
    
    - It receives both **Excitatory** ("Fire!") and **Inhibitory** ("Don't Fire!") signals.
        
    - The dendrite adds these signals up spatially and temporally. If the "Yes" votes outweigh the "No" votes enough to hit a specific voltage threshold at the base of the cell, the neuron fires.
