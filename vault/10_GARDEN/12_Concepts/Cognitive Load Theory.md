# ⚛️ Cognitive Load Theory

**🔗 Related:** [[Working Memory]], [[John Sweller]], [[Desirable Difficulty]], [[Expertise Reversal Effect]], [[Scaffolding]], [[Split-Attention Effect]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> A theory developed by **John Sweller** in the 1980s which suggests that since working memory has a limited capacity, instructional design should be used to reduce the "load" that does not contribute directly to learning, thereby leaving more capacity for the "load" that does.

- **The Core Premise:** If the total cognitive load required by a task exceeds the learner's working memory capacity, learning will fail.
    
- **The Goal:** To align the presentation of information with the architecture of the human mind.

### 📝 Key Insight
* ### 📝 Key Insight

- **The Script-Writer's Rule:** In your YouTube videos, every second of "fancy animation" that doesn't directly explain the concept is **Extraneous Load**.
    
- To be an effective teacher, you must treat your viewer's **[[Working Memory]]** like a bank account with a very low daily limit. If you spend that "currency" on flashy transitions or loud background music, they won't have enough left to buy the "Insight" you're selling.

### 📐 The Three Types of Cognitive Load

Sweller divides the "load" on your brain into three distinct categories. Effective teaching (and your YouTube script) should balance these carefully:

1. **Intrinsic Load:** The inherent difficulty of the material itself.
    
    - _Example:_ Learning $2+2$ has a low intrinsic load; learning Quantum Physics has a high one.
        
    - _Management:_ You cannot "remove" this, but you can manage it by breaking complex ideas into smaller **[[Chunks]]**.
        
2. **Extraneous Load:** The "Bad" Load. This is the mental effort required to process poorly designed instructions.
    
    - _Example:_ Having to look at a diagram on page 5 while reading the explanation on page 10. The brain wastes energy "moving" between the two.
        
    - _Management:_ Eliminate distractions and keep related information physically close (**[[Spatial Contiguity]]**).
        
3. **Germane Load:** The "Good" Load. This is the mental effort dedicated to processing, constructing, and automating **Schemas** (mental models).
    
    - _Example:_ The effort you put into relating a new concept to something you already know.
        
    - _Management:_ Maximize this by encouraging **[[Active Recall]]**.
        

### 🧪 Key Effects & Pitfalls

- **The Split-Attention Effect:** Occurs when learners are forced to integrate two separate sources of information (e.g., a video with text on one side and a diagram on the other). This spikes Extraneous Load.
    
- **The Redundancy Effect:** Providing the same information in two different formats (e.g., reading a slide out loud word-for-word). The brain tries to coordinate the two identical streams, which wastes working memory.
    
- **The Expertise Reversal Effect:** What is "Desirable Difficulty" for an expert is often "Extraneous Load" for a beginner.
    
    - _Beginners_ need heavy **[[Scaffolding]]** and worked examples.
        
    - _Experts_ learn better with open-ended problems; giving them too much guidance actually _hurts_ their performance.
