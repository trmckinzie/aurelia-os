# ⚛️ Brain

**🔗 Related:** [[The Mind]], [[Neuron]], [[Action Potential]], [[Neuroplasticity]], [[Prefrontal Cortex]], [[The Hard Problem]], [[Predictive Processing]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The physical, three-pound mass of fat, water, and protein located in the skull that acts as the command center for the central nervous system.

- **The Specs:** It contains roughly **86 billion neurons** and an equal number of glial cells (support cells). These neurons are connected by over **100 trillion synapses**, creating a computational network of staggering density.
    
- **The Expense:** Despite making up only ~2% of the body's weight, it consumes **20% of the body's energy** (glucose and oxygen). It is an incredibly expensive biological machine, which is why mechanisms like **[[Habit]]** evolved to save power.

### 📝 Key Insight
**The Predictive Engine:** The classical view of the brain (from the Cognitive Revolution) was a passive computer: _Input $\rightarrow$ Processing $\rightarrow$ Output_.

- Modern neuroscience is shifting to **Predictive Processing (Active Inference)**. The brain is locked inside a dark, silent skull. It doesn't "see" the world; it hallucinates a model of the world _from the inside out_, and then uses the trickle of sensory data coming from the eyes and ears only to correct its errors.
    
- Your perception of reality is just a controlled hallucination generated by the brain. When the hallucination matches reality, we call it perception. When it doesn't, we call it a delusion.

### 🔬 The Scientific View (The "Wetware")

From a biological perspective, the brain is an evolved survival mechanism. It is roughly organized by evolutionary age:

1. **The Brainstem & Cerebellum (The Engine Room):** The oldest structures. They manage unconscious, vital functions like heart rate, breathing, and basic motor coordination.
2. **The Limbic System (The Animal Brain):** Includes the Amygdala and Hippocampus. It handles emotion, memory, and the "Four F's" of survival (feeding, fighting, fleeing, and reproduction).
3. **The Neocortex (The Human Computer):** The wrinkled outer layer. This is where high-level processing occurs. It is divided into four lobes:

- _Frontal:_ Executive function, planning, impulse control (**[[Prefrontal Cortex]]**).
    
- _Parietal:_ Sensory integration and spatial navigation.
    
- _Temporal:_ Auditory processing and language comprehension.
    
- _Occipital:_ Visual processing.

### 🔭 The Philosophical Paradox

If the brain is just meat and chemistry, how does it produce the "Mind"?

- **The Explanatory Gap:** Science can trace the exact pathway of light hitting the retina, traveling up the optic nerve, and firing neurons in the occipital lobe. But no equation can explain how those physical electrical spikes transform into the subjective, private _feeling_ of seeing the color red. This is **[[The Hard Problem]]** of consciousness.
    
- **The Free Will Problem:** The brain obeys the laws of physics and chemistry. If every neural firing is caused by a previous physical event (a chain reaction of ions), where is the room for "Choice"? Experiments (like the Libet experiment) show that the brain initiates motor actions _before_ the person is consciously aware of deciding to act. Is consciousness just a passenger that thinks it's the driver?
