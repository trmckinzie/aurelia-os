# ⚛️ Metacognition

**🔗 Related:** [[System 1 vs System 2 (Dual-Process Theory)]], [[Deliberate Practice]], [[The Illusion of Competence]], [[Working Memory]], [[Prefrontal Cortex]], [[Dunning-Kruger Effect]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> Simply put, it is "thinking about thinking." It is the higher-order cognitive process used to plan, monitor, and assess one's own understanding and performance.

- **The Origin:** The term was coined by developmental psychologist **John Flavell** in the 1970s.
    
- **The Function:** It is the "Ghost in the Machine." While normal cognition allows you to solve a math problem, _metacognition_ is the voice in your head that says, "Wait, I'm doing this wrong. Let me try a different strategy."

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The Dunning-Kruger Effect:** This famous cognitive bias is ultimately a massive failure of metacognition. Beginners overestimate their abilities precisely because they lack the metacognitive knowledge required to realize how much they _don't_ know.

- _The Paradox of Expertise:_ Becoming an expert in a field actually requires two simultaneous processes: building the skill itself, and building the metacognitive lens to critique the skill.

### 📐 The Two Pillars of Metacognition

Flavell divided this "managerial" skill into two distinct components:

1. **Metacognitive Knowledge:** What you know about yourself as a learner, the task at hand, and the strategies available.
    
    - _Self-Knowledge:_ "I am terrible at focusing in the afternoon."
        
    - _Task Knowledge:_ "This chapter on calculus is going to be much harder than the last one."
        
    - _Strategy Knowledge:_ "I know that using **[[Active Recall]]** is better than just re-reading."
        
2. **Metacognitive Regulation:** The actual, real-time adjustments you make while learning.
    
    - _Planning:_ Setting goals before studying.
        
    - _Monitoring:_ Checking your understanding mid-task (e.g., stopping at the end of a page and asking, "Did I actually understand what I just read?").
        
    - _Evaluating:_ Reviewing your performance after a test to see where your strategies failed.
        

### ⚙️ The Bandwidth Manager (System 2's Boss)

In the context of **[[System 1 vs System 2 (Dual-Process Theory)]]**, Metacognition is the alarm bell that wakes up System 2.

- Most of the time, we cruise on autopilot (System 1). If you lack metacognition, you will happily coast on autopilot right off a cliff, convinced you are doing great (this is the **[[Illusion of Competence]]**).
    
- Strong metacognitive skills act as a constant monitoring system. When it detects an anomaly or an error, it manually overrides System 1, boots up System 2, and forces the brain into the uncomfortable, energy-draining state of **[[Deliberate Practice]]**.
