# ⚛️ Myelination

**🔗 Related:** [[Axon]], [[Glial Cells]], [[Action Potential]], [[Saltatory Conduction]], [[Neuroplasticity]], [[White Matter]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The process of forming a **myelin sheath** around the nerve fiber (axon) to allow nerve impulses to move more quickly.

- **The Composition:** Myelin is a fatty, lipid-rich substance that acts as an electrical insulator.
    
- **The Manufacturers:** > * In the **Central Nervous System (CNS)**: Created by **Oligodendrocytes**.
    
    - In the **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)**: Created by **Schwann Cells**.

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The Bandwidth Constraint:** Myelination is the brain's way of solving the "Space vs. Speed" trade-off. To get high speeds without myelin, axons would have to be incredibly thick (like the Giant Squid Axon). Myelin allows the brain to pack millions of high-speed "wires" into a very small skull.

### 🏎️ The Physics: Saltatory Conduction

Without myelin, an electrical signal must crawl down the entire length of the axon membrane. With myelin, the signal "jumps."

- **The Nodes of Ranvier:** These are the uninsulated gaps between myelin segments. They are packed with voltage-gated sodium channels.
    
- **The "Jump":** Instead of a continuous wave, the electrical charge skips from node to node. This is called **Saltatory Conduction** (from the Latin _saltare_, to hop).
    
- **The Result:** It increases signal speed from roughly **1 meter per second** (unmyelinated) to over **100 meters per second** (myelinated).

### 🧠 Developmental & Functional Impact

1. **The "White Matter":** In a brain cross-section, "Gray Matter" consists of cell bodies, while "White Matter" consists of myelinated axons. The white color is literally the fat of the insulation.
    
2. **The Learning Loop:** Recent research suggests that **Deep Practice** triggers more myelination. Every time you repeat a skill, you "wrap" that specific neural circuit in more insulation, making the skill faster, more accurate, and less energy-intensive. This is the physical basis of "muscle memory."
    
3. **The Maturity Timeline:** Myelination starts in the womb but continues into your mid-20s. Crucially, the **Prefrontal Cortex** (responsible for judgment and impulse control) is one of the last areas to be fully myelinated, which explains much of adolescent behavior.
