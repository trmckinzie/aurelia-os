# ⚛️ Pre vs Post Synaptic Changes

**🔗 Related:** [[Neurotransmitter]], [[Hormone]], [[Long Term Potentiation (LTP)]], [[Long Term Depression (LTD)]], [[Robert Sapolsky]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The two fundamental ways a neural pathway can change how effectively it transmits a signal across the synaptic gap.

- **The Anatomy:** A synapse involves a "speaker" (the pre-synaptic neuron releasing neurotransmitters) and a "listener" (the post-synaptic neuron receiving them via receptors).
    
- **Pre-Synaptic Changes:** This changes the _volume_ of the signal. The pre-synaptic neuron releases either more or less of a given **[[Neurotransmitter]]** (like glutamate or dopamine) into the gap.
    
- **Post-Synaptic Changes:** This changes the _sensitivity_ of the listener. The post-synaptic neuron physically builds and inserts new receptors (more microphones) to catch the signal more easily, or absorbs existing ones to mute the signal.

### 📝 Key Insight
- **The Metabolic Cost of Learning:** Through the lens of "The Economics of Cognitive Bandwidth," there is a massive energetic difference between these two changes.
    
- **Short-Term vs Long-Term Bandwidth:** Pre-synaptic changes (dumping more neurotransmitter) are fast and metabolically cheap, but they are temporary. This is how **[[System 1 vs System 2|System 1]]** reacts in the moment (like an adrenaline spike). Post-synaptic changes, however, require the cell to literally transcribe DNA, synthesize new proteins, and physically alter its architecture. This is highly metabolically expensive, but it represents a permanent hardware upgrade.
    
- **The Biological Basis of Habit:** When you force your **[[The Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)]]** to repeat a difficult task over and over, you are forcing a post-synaptic change. Once the new receptors are built, the behavior becomes computationally "cheaper" to execute in the future, eventually becoming a fully automated, low-bandwidth habit.
