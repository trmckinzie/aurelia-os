# ⚛️ Cognition (Neuroscience)

**🔗 Related:** [[Neural Network]], [[Working Memory]], [[Dual-Process Theory]], [[Prefrontal Cortex]], [[Information Processing Theory]], [[Emergence]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The mental action or process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through thought, experience, and the senses. In the context of neuroscience, cognition is not a mystical force; it is the high-level, functional output of billions of interconnected neurons computing information in parallel.

- **The Metaphor:** If the brain is the physical computer hardware, and the **[[Neural Network]]** is the operating system architecture, then Cognition is the active software application running on the screen.
    
- **The Core Mechanism:** It encompasses all aspects of intellectual function: perception, attention, memory, language, problem-solving, and decision-making.

### 📝 Key Insight
*  The Economics of Cognition: Tying this back to your vault's grand theme, true "Cognition" (conscious, deliberate thought) is the most metabolically expensive process in the human body. The brain consumes 20% of your daily caloric intake despite being only 2% of your body weight.
    
- Because active cognition is so expensive, the brain is fundamentally designed to avoid it whenever possible. This is the biological root of **[[Cognitive Bias]]**, **[[Heuristic]]**, and **[[Dual-Process Theory]],[[System 1]]**. We only engage in true, heavy-lifting cognition when our automated survival scripts fail.


### 📐 The Information Processing Pipeline

Cognitive neuroscientists generally view cognition through an "Information Processing" model, which breaks down the magic of "thinking" into distinct, measurable stages of data manipulation:

1. **Sensory Input (Transduction):** The world provides physical stimuli (photons, sound waves). Sensory organs translate this physical energy into electrical **[[Action Potential|Action Potentials]]**.
    
2. **Perception & Pattern Recognition:** The raw electrical data hits the early sensory cortices (like the Occipital Lobe for vision). The brain uses **[[Neural Network|Parallel Processing]]** to extract features (lines, edges, colors) and matches them against stored **[[Schema Theory|Schemas]]** to identify objects.
    
3. **Attention & Working Memory:** The brain cannot process everything. **Executive Function** (driven by the Prefrontal Cortex) acts as a spotlight, filtering out noise and holding relevant data in the active workspace (**[[Working Memory]]**) for manipulation.
    
4. **Integration & Action (Executive Output):** The brain compares the current data with long-term memory, simulates potential future outcomes, and generates a motor or verbal command.
    

### 🌌 Emergence: The Ghost in the Machine

The greatest mystery in cognitive neuroscience is how subjective, conscious thought arises from unconscious, wet biological matter.

The prevailing scientific answer is **Emergence**—the idea that complex systems display properties that their individual parts do not possess.

- A single water molecule is not "wet." Wetness is an emergent property of billions of water molecules interacting.
    
- A single **[[Neuron]]** does not have a "thought." It just fires an electrical spark. However, when 86 billion neurons fire in highly synchronized, heavily plastic networks, the resulting emergent property is a memory, a calculation, or the feeling of sadness.
