# ⚛️ Axon

**🔗 Related:** [[Neuron]], [[Action Potential]], [[Myelination]], [[Node of Ranvier]], [[Axon Hillock]], [[Synaptic Vesicles]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> A long, slender projection of a nerve cell (neuron) that typically conducts electrical impulses away from the neuron's cell body (soma) to other neurons, muscles, or glands.

- **The Anatomy of a Cable:** While dendrites are short and bushy, the axon is a single long cable. Most are microscopic, but some (like those running from your spine to your big toe) can be over a meter long.
    
- **The Polarity:** In a standard neuron, information is a one-way street: **Dendrite $\to$ Soma $\to$ Axon.**

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The All-or-Nothing Law:** The axon operates on binary logic. An axon doesn't send "weak" or "strong" electrical pulses. It either fires a full-strength Action Potential or it doesn't fire at all ($1$ or $0$).

- _How do we feel intensity (like a hard vs. soft touch)?_ The brain doesn't look at the _voltage_ of the pulse; it looks at the **frequency** (how many pulses per second). This is a biological version of **Frequency Modulation (FM)**.

### ⚡ The Engineering of the Signal

The axon is not just a "wire"; it is a sophisticated self-powering relay system.

1. **The Trigger (Axon Hillock):** This is the junction between the cell body and the axon. It acts as the "Gatekeeper." It monitors the electrical voltage from the dendrites; only if the voltage hits a specific threshold does it trigger an **[[Action Potential]]**.
    
2. **The Insulation (Myelin):** As noted in your [[Myelination]] entry, the axon is often wrapped in fatty glial cells. This converts the axon from a slow, leaking cable into a high-speed "jump-based" conductor.
    
3. **The Terminals (Axon Terminals):** At the very end, the axon branches out into terminals. This is where the electrical signal is converted back into a chemical signal (neurotransmitters) to cross the **[[Synapse]]**.
    

### 🔍 Scientific Distinction: Axonal Transport

Because the axon can be so long, it has its own internal "railway system." The cell body produces proteins and nutrients that the distant axon tip needs to survive.

- **Anterograde Transport:** Kinesin proteins "walk" nutrients from the soma _down_ to the terminal.
    
- **Retrograde Transport:** Dynein proteins carry waste or signaling molecules from the terminal _back_ to the soma.
