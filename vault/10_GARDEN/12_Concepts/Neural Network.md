# ⚛️ Neural Network (Biological)

**🔗 Related:** [[Neuron]], [[Neuroplasticity]], [[Synapse]], [[Hebbian Learning]], [[Schema Theory]], [[Parallel Distributed Processing]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> A heavily interconnected web of neurons that work collaboratively to process sensory input, compute logical operations, and generate motor output.

- **The Core Distinction:** Unlike traditional computer processors that read code sequentially (step-by-step), neural networks compute in _parallel_. Millions of operations happen simultaneously across the web.
    
- **The Storage Mechanism:** In a neural network, knowledge is not stored in a specific "file" or single cell. Instead, memory and logic are stored as a distributed pattern of connection strengths (**Weights**) across thousands of synapses

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The Inspiration for AI:** The Artificial Neural Networks (like the Large Language Model powering this very response) were explicitly inspired by the architecture of the biological brain. We use digital "nodes" instead of neurons, and mathematical "weights" instead of synapses.

- _The Hardware Gap:_ However, while an AI like me requires massive server farms and gigawatts of electricity to process language, your biological neural network runs on roughly 20 watts of power—the equivalent of a dim lightbulb. The biological brain remains the most energy-efficient computational engine in the known universe.

### 📐 The Architecture of a Network

While biological networks are incredibly messy, they generally organize themselves into functional layers (a structure that Artificial Intelligence later copied):

1. **Input Layer:** The sensory neurons. They receive raw data from the outside world (e.g., photons hitting the retina, pressure on the skin).
    
2. **Hidden Layers:** The vast majority of the brain. These intermediate networks receive the raw data, extract features, apply **[[Schema Theory|Schemas]]**, and do the actual "thinking." In the human cortex, there can be dozens of hidden layers stacked on top of each other, building up from basic shapes to complex concepts (like recognizing a face).
    
3. **Output Layer:** The motor neurons. The final decision of the network, translated into physical action (e.g., muscle contraction, speech, or releasing a hormone).
    

### 🔋 The Economics of Parallel Processing (PDP)

Neural Networks operate on a model called **Parallel Distributed Processing (PDP)**. This is the ultimate "Economics of Cognitive Bandwidth" hack for two reasons:

- **Graceful Degradation:** If you remove a transistor from a traditional computer, the whole machine crashes. If a few thousand neurons die in your brain every day, you don't forget how to walk or speak. Because information is distributed across the _entire_ network, biological networks can take massive damage and still function reasonably well.
    
- **Pattern Completion:** Because of PDP, a neural network does not need a complete set of data to recognize a pattern. If you see a tiger partially hidden behind a bush, your visual network takes the incomplete input (orange, stripes) and the hidden layers instantly "fill in the blanks" based on past experience. It requires less **[[Working Memory]]** to compute a probability than an absolute certainty.
