# ⚛️ Sustained Stress

**🔗 Related:** [[Hormone]], [[Cortisol]], [[Evolutionary Mismatch]], [[The Amygdala]], [[Allostatic Load]], [[Robert Sapolsky]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> A physiological state where the body's acute stress response (fight-or-flight) is activated chronically over long periods, rather than in brief, life-or-death bursts.

- **Sapolsky’s Model:** In _Behave_ (and his earlier work _Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers_), Sapolsky explains that the mammalian stress response is perfectly designed for short-term physical crises (e.g., sprinting away from a lion). It shuts down non-essential systems (digestion, reproduction, tissue repair) to flood the muscles with energy.
    
- **The Mechanism:** When the brain perceives a threat, the hypothalamus signals the adrenal glands to release **[[Hormone|Hormones]]** like adrenaline and **[[Cortisol]]**. Under normal conditions, once the threat passes, cortisol levels drop, and the parasympathetic nervous system (rest-and-digest) takes over. In sustained stress, the "off switch" never fully engages.

### 📝 Key Insight
- **The Cost of Psychological Time Travel:** Through the lens of "The Economics of Cognitive Bandwidth," sustained stress is the ultimate **[[Evolutionary Mismatch]]**. A zebra only feels stress when it is actively being hunted. Humans, however, possess a **[[The Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)|Prefrontal Cortex]]** capable of simulating the future and remembering the past. We can trigger the exact same biological panic response just by _thinking_ about a mortgage payment or a social slight.
    
- **Hardware Degradation:** Because the stress response is so metabolically expensive, running it continuously physically damages the hardware. Chronic cortisol exposure literally shrinks the hippocampus (memory) and the Prefrontal Cortex (executive function), while enlarging **[[The Amygdala]]** (fear detection). Sustained stress doesn't just make you feel bad; it aggressively degrades your **[[System 2]]** computational capacity, locking you into reactive, **[[System 1 vs System 2|System 1]]** behavior.
