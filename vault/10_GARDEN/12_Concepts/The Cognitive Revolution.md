# ⚛️ The Cognitive Revolution

**🔗 Related:** [[Behaviorism]], [[George Miller]], [[Noam Chomsky]], [[Information Theory]], [[Computational Theory of Mind]], [[B.F. Skinner]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> An intellectual movement in the 1950s that birthed the interdisciplinary field of Cognitive Science. It was a direct rebellion against **Behaviorism** (the dominant psychological theory at the time), shifting the scientific focus from _observable behavior_ to _internal mental processes_.

- **The Old Paradigm (Behaviorism):** The mind is a "Black Box." It is unscientific to talk about thoughts, memories, or beliefs because you can't measure them. Science should only measure the Input (Stimulus) and the Output (Response).
    
- **The New Paradigm (Cognitivism):** The mind is an **Information Processing System**. Thanks to the invention of the computer, scientists finally had a vocabulary and a metaphor to measure the invisible: "Working Memory," "Encoding," "Retrieval," and "Processing."

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The Ghost is Just Code:** The revolution’s greatest achievement was making the mind safe for materialistic science. You don't need a magical "ghost in the machine" to explain human intelligence; you just need incredibly complex information processing. By treating the mind like a computer, we finally gained the tools to reverse-engineer it.

### 🏛️ The Conception: The 1956 MIT Symposium

Psychologist **George Miller** famously dated the precise birth of Cognitive Science to September 11, 1956, during the "Special Interest Group in Information Theory" symposium at MIT. For the first time, researchers from entirely different fields realized they were all working on the exact same problem:

1. **Psychology:** George Miller presented his famous paper on the limits of short-term memory (_"The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two"_), proving the brain has quantifiable "channel capacity" constraints.
    
2. **Linguistics:** A young **Noam Chomsky** presented his theory of syntax, arguing that language is not just a learned habit (as Skinner claimed), but a hard-wired, generative, mathematical structure in the brain.
    
3. **Computer Science:** Allen Newell and Herbert Simon presented the "Logic Theory Machine," the first artificial intelligence program capable of proving mathematical theorems.
    

Miller left that symposium with the profound realization that psychology, linguistics, and computer science were actually studying the same underlying phenomenon: **Computation.**

### 🌍 Why It Matters (The Importance)

- **Historically:** It ended the 50-year reign of B.F. Skinner's radical behaviorism. It proved that humans are not just passive dogs drooling at bells; we are active processors of information.
    
- **Scientifically:** It forced an unprecedented interdisciplinary synthesis. Psychologists realized they needed computer scientists to build models, linguists to understand language, and neuroscientists to find the hardware. This created the classic "Cognitive Hexagon" (Psychology, Philosophy, Linguistics, Anthropology, Neuroscience, Artificial Intelligence).
    
- **Philosophically:** It rescued "Mental States" from the realm of magic and soul. Before this, philosophers struggled to explain how a non-physical "thought" could cause a physical action. The Cognitive Revolution solved this by introducing the **[[Computational Theory of Mind]]**: The brain is the hardware, and the mind is the software. Software isn't physical, but it is entirely real, governed by logic, and runs on a physical machine.
