# ⚛️ Visual Imagery

**🔗 Related:** [[Working Memory]], [[Visuospatial Sketchpad]], [[Dual-Coding Theory]], [[Aphantasia]], [[Mental Rotation]], [[Top-Down Processing]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The cognitive process of representing sensory information in the mind when that information is not currently being received by the sense organs.

- **The Experience:** It is "seeing" with the mind's eye. While most research focuses on the visual, imagery can be multi-modal (auditory, olfactory, tactile).
    
- **The Function:** Imagery is not just a "movie" in the head; it is a functional tool used for **Problem Solving** (e.g., "Will this sofa fit through that door?"), **Memory** (e.g., Method of Loci), and **Navigation**.

### 📝 Key Insight
- **Mental Rotation:** One of the most robust findings in cognitive science is that we can physically rotate objects in our minds. If you are shown two 3D shapes and asked if they are the same, your reaction time is perfectly correlated with how many degrees you would have to rotate the physical object to match it.
    
- **Aphantasia:** A significant discovery in the last decade is that not everyone has this "software." People with Aphantasia have no "mind's eye" and cannot visualize anything, despite having perfectly functioning eyes and memory. They solve spatial problems using purely **Propositional** logic.

### ⚖️ The Great Imagery Debate: Analog vs. Propositional

In the 1970s, a famous debate erupted between **Stephen Kosslyn** and **Zenon Pylyshyn** regarding how the brain actually stores these images:

1. **Analog/Depictive Position (Kosslyn):** Mental images are like actual pictures. They preserve the spatial geometry of the object.
    
    - _Evidence:_ **Mental Scanning** experiments. When subjects are asked to "scan" from one end of a mental map to another, it takes them longer to reach distant points than near points—just like moving your physical eyes across a real map.
        
2. **Propositional Position (Pylyshyn):** Mental images are actually just "under the hood" code. They are stored as abstract, language-like descriptions (e.g., "The cat is UNDER the table"). The "image" we feel we see is just an epiphenomenon (a byproduct) that doesn't actually do the work.
    

### 🧪 Neural Overlap: Perception vs. Imagery

Modern fMRI studies have largely settled the debate in favor of the **Analog** view.

- **Shared Hardware:** When you see a real tree, your **[[Occipital Lobe]]** (Primary Visual Cortex) fires. When you _imagine_ a tree with your eyes closed, the exact same areas of the brain fire, though with less intensity.
    
- **Top-Down Flow:** Perception is **Bottom-Up** (Eyes $\to$ Brain). Imagery is **Top-Down** (Frontal Lobe/Memory $\to$ Visual Cortex). Your brain essentially "tricks" its own visual system into seeing what memory provides.
