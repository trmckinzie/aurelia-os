# ⚛️ Negentropy

**🔗 Related:** [[Entropy]], [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]], [[Dissipative Structures]], [[Homeostasis]], [[Autopoiesis]], [[Schrödinger's "What is Life?"]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> Short for **Negative Entropy**. It is a measure of order, organization, and structure within a system.

- **The Origin:** Coined by physicist **Erwin Schrödinger** in his famous book _What is Life?_ (1944). He argued that living things avoid the rapid decay into inert equilibrium (death) by continuously extracting "negative entropy" from their environment.
    
- **The Mechanism:** To stay alive (highly ordered), an organism must consume low-entropy energy (food, sunlight) and export high-entropy waste (heat, CO2).
    
- **In Information Theory:** Negentropy is equivalent to information. It represents the _presence_ of structure and distinctiveness against a background of noise.

### 📝 Key Insight
* **Life as a Local Rebellion:** The Second Law of Thermodynamics states that the total entropy of the universe must always increase. Life does not violate this law; it circumvents it locally.

- We build complex, ordered bodies (decreasing local entropy) by paying a tax to the universe in the form of heat (increasing global entropy).
    
- Therefore, a human being is essentially a **Negentropy Machine**—a localized island of order floating in a river of increasing disorder.
