# ⚛️ Cognitive Bias

**🔗 Related:** [[Dual-Process Theory]], [[Heuristics]], [[Daniel Kahneman]], [[Amos Tversky]], [[Metacognition]], [[Evolutionary Mismatch]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> A systematic pattern of deviation from norm or rationality in judgment. It occurs when individuals create their own "subjective reality" based on their perception of the input, dictating their behavior in the world, often leading to perceptual distortion, inaccurate judgment, or illogical interpretation.

- **The Cause:** Biases are rarely the result of a "broken" brain. They are the mathematical cost of **efficiency**. Because the brain's bandwidth is strictly limited (**[[Working Memory]]**), **System 1** relies on fast, energy-saving rules of thumb (**Heuristics**). A bias is simply a heuristic misfiring in a complex environment.
    
- **The Origin:** First identified and mapped extensively by Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman in the 1970s.

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The Evolutionary Mismatch:** Our brains evolved on the African savanna, where making a fast, "good enough" decision (Type I Error: mistaking a stick for a snake) kept you alive. Today, we use that exact same hardware to navigate the stock market, vote in elections, and consume social media algorithms. The hardware is optimized for a world that no longer exists, making our survival instincts our greatest intellectual liabilities.

### 📐 The Four Evolutionary Drivers (Why we have them)

If you look at the hundreds of identified cognitive biases (often visualized in the massive "[[Cognitive Bias Codex]]"), they all exist to solve one of four fundamental bandwidth problems:

1. **Too Much Information:** The brain must filter noise. We notice things that are primed in memory or repeated often. _(e.g., **Availability Heuristic**: Believing airplane crashes are highly probable just because they are heavily reported in the news)._
    
2. **Not Enough Meaning:** The brain abhors a vacuum. It connects dots and fills in gaps using existing **[[Schema Theory|Schemas]]**. _(e.g., **Confirmation Bias**: We actively seek out and overweight information that confirms what we already believe, while ignoring data that contradicts it)._
    
3. **The Need to Act Fast:** We are constrained by time and danger. We favor simple, immediate options over complex, delayed ones. _(e.g., **Sunk Cost Fallacy**: Continuing a failing project just because we've already invested time/money into it)._
    
4. **What Should We Remember?:** We can't store everything. We discard specifics to form generalities. _(e.g., **The Peak-End Rule**: We judge an experience largely based on how we felt at its most intense point and at its end, rather than the total sum of the experience)._
    

### 🧪 The Role of Metacognition

Biases occur entirely within **System 1** (the autopilot). They are completely invisible to the person experiencing them in the moment.

You cannot "will" yourself out of a cognitive bias just by being smart. The only defense is a robust **[[Metacognition]]**—the ability to recognize the environmental triggers of a bias and manually force **System 2** to boot up and review the math.
