# ⚛️ Cognitive Bias Codex

**🔗 Related:** [[Cognitive Bias]], [[Heuristics]], [[Working Memory]], [[System 1 vs System 2 (Dual-Process Theory)]], [[Evolutionary Mismatch]], [[Schema Theory]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> A comprehensive visual taxonomy of human irrationality. Originally categorized by Buster Benson and visually designed by John Manoogian III, the Codex maps out over 180 recognized cognitive biases, grouping them by the specific evolutionary "bandwidth problem" they are trying to solve.

- **The Artifact:** It is typically depicted as a massive, radial circle, showing how individual biases (like the _Sunk Cost Fallacy_ or the _Dunning-Kruger Effect_) branch out from central, structural constraints of the brain.
    
- **The Purpose:** It shifts the perspective of biases from "random human errors" to a highly organized, predictable system of algorithmic tradeoffs.

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The Codex is a Feature List, Not a Bug Report:** When you look at the Codex, it looks like a damning indictment of human intelligence. But through the lens of "The Economics of Cognitive Bandwidth," it is actually a masterpiece of engineering. The Codex proves that the brain prioritizes _survival and speed_ over _objective truth_.
    
- **The Metacognitive Tool:** You cannot memorize all 180 biases. However, by memorizing the _four core problems_ of the Codex, you build a powerful **[[Metacognition|Metacognitive]]** alarm system. When you feel overwhelmed, uncertain, rushed, or nostalgic, you immediately know your **System 1** is about to deploy a heuristic that might fail.

### 📐 The Four Quadrants of the Codex

The genius of the Codex is that it distills 180+ confusing psychological terms into four fundamental bottlenecks of human cognition. (Notice how these perfectly align with your **[[Working Memory]]** and **[[Cognitive Load Theory]]** notes):

1. **Problem 1: Too Much Information (The Filter)**
    
    - _The Constraint:_ The universe has infinite data; **Working Memory** can only hold ~4 items.
        
    - _The Hack:_ We filter aggressively. We notice things that are primed, repeated, bizarre, or emotionally striking.
        
    - _The Biases (Examples):_ _Availability Heuristic, Baader-Meinhof Phenomenon (Frequency Illusion), Confirmation Bias._
        
2. **Problem 2: Not Enough Meaning (The Storyteller)**
    
    - _The Constraint:_ We cannot survive in a state of constant ambiguity.
        
    - _The Hack:_ We connect the dots, fill in the blanks using existing **Schemas**, and project our current mindset into the past and future.
        
    - _The Biases (Examples):_ _Halo Effect, Gambler's Fallacy, Hindsight Bias._
        
3. **Problem 3: Need to Act Fast (The Engine)**
    
    - _The Constraint:_ Thinking (**System 2**) takes time and glucose. Predators do not wait for you to do math.
        
    - _The Hack:_ We favor the immediate, the relatable, and the status quo. We assume we are right to save time.
        
    - _The Biases (Examples):_ _Sunk Cost Fallacy, Dunning-Kruger Effect, Overconfidence Effect._
        
4. **Problem 4: What Should We Remember? (The Hard Drive)**
    
    - _The Constraint:_ We cannot store a 1:1 video recording of our lives.
        
    - _The Hack:_ We edit and compress memories. We save the "gist" of an event, the emotional peak, and the ending, discarding the rest.
        
    - _The Biases (Examples):_ _Peak-End Rule, Misinformation Effect, Negativity Bias._

>Here is a comprehensive, alphabetical list of many of the most prominent cognitive biases recognized in the Cognitive Bias Codex.

1. **Action Bias:** The tendency to act in an ambiguous situation, even when doing nothing is the more logical option.
    
2. **Actor-Observer Bias:** The tendency to attribute other people's behavior to internal causes (their personality) while attributing our own actions to external causes (our circumstances).
    
3. **Ambiguity Effect:** The tendency to avoid options for which the probability of a favorable outcome is unknown or missing.
    
4. **Anchoring Effect:** The tendency to rely too heavily on the first piece of information offered (the "anchor") when making decisions.
    
5. **Apophenia:** The tendency to perceive meaningful connections or patterns between unrelated things.
    
6. **Attentional Bias:** The tendency for our perception to be affected by our recurring thoughts at the time.
    
7. **Availability Heuristic:** The tendency to overestimate the likelihood of events that are easily recalled or possess greater "availability" in memory.
    
8. **Baader-Meinhof Phenomenon (Frequency Illusion):** The illusion in which something you recently learned suddenly seems to appear everywhere with improbable frequency.
    
9. **Backfire Effect:** The reaction to disconfirming evidence by actually strengthening one's previous beliefs rather than changing them.
    
10. **Bandwagon Effect:** The tendency to adopt beliefs or behaviors simply because many other people are doing the same.
    
11. **Barnum Effect (Forer Effect):** The tendency to give high accuracy ratings to vague, general personality descriptions (like horoscopes) that supposedly are tailored specifically for you.
    
12. **Base Rate Fallacy:** The tendency to ignore general, statistical information in favor of specific, anecdotal information.
    
13. **Belief Bias:** The tendency to judge the strength of a logical argument based on how believable the conclusion is, rather than the logic itself.
    
14. **Bystander Effect:** The tendency to assume someone else will intervene in an emergency situation when other people are present.
    
15. **Cheerleader Effect:** The tendency for individuals to appear more physically attractive when they are in a group rather than in isolation.
    
16. **Confirmation Bias:** The tendency to search for, interpret, and remember information in a way that confirms one's preexisting beliefs.
    
17. **Curse of Knowledge:** The difficulty well-informed people have when trying to think about or explain problems from the perspective of lesser-informed people.
    
18. **Decoy Effect:** The tendency to change your preference between two options when presented with a third, strategically inferior "decoy" option.
    
19. **Dunning-Kruger Effect:** The tendency for unskilled individuals to overestimate their abilities, and highly skilled individuals to underestimate their abilities.
    
20. **Empathy Gap:** The tendency to underestimate the influence of strong emotions or visceral states (like hunger or anger) on our own or others' decision-making.
    
21. **Endowment Effect:** The tendency to demand much more to give up an object you already own than you would be willing to pay to acquire it.
    
22. **Framing Effect:** The tendency to draw different conclusions from the exact same information depending on how it is presented (e.g., "90% fat-free" vs. "10% fat").
    
23. **Fundamental Attribution Error:** The tendency to over-emphasize personality traits to explain someone else's behavior while under-emphasizing situational factors.
    
24. **Gambler's Fallacy:** The belief that independent, future probabilities are altered by past events (e.g., "The coin landed on heads five times, so tails is 'due'").
    
25. **Halo Effect:** The tendency for your overall impression of a person to heavily influence how you feel and think about their specific character traits.
    
26. **Hindsight Bias:** The "I-knew-it-all-along" effect; the tendency to see past events as being highly predictable after they have already occurred.
    
27. **Hostile Attribution Bias:** The tendency to interpret others' ambiguous behaviors as having hostile or malicious intent.
    
28. **IKEA Effect:** The tendency to place a disproportionately high value on objects or projects that you partially assembled or built yourself.
    
29. **Illusion of Control:** The tendency to overestimate your degree of influence over external, random, or uncontrollable events.
    
30. **Illusion of Truth Effect:** The tendency to believe false information to be correct simply after being repeatedly exposed to it.
    
31. **Illusory Correlation:** Inaccurately perceiving a relationship or correlation between two completely unrelated events.
    
32. **Information Bias:** The tendency to obsessively seek information even when it cannot realistically affect the action or decision.
    
33. **Loss Aversion:** The psychological phenomenon where the pain of losing something feels twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining that exact same thing.
    
34. **Negativity Bias:** The tendency to register, dwell on, and recall negative events, memories, or insults much more strongly than positive ones.
    
35. **Normalcy Bias:** The refusal to plan for or react to a disaster that has never happened before, assuming things will always function normally.
    
36. **Omission Bias:** The tendency to judge harmful actions as worse or less moral than equally harmful inactions (omissions).
    
37. **Optimism Bias:** The tendency to overestimate the likelihood of positive outcomes happening to you, and underestimate the likelihood of negative outcomes.
    
38. **Ostrich Effect:** The tendency to literally or metaphorically "bury your head in the sand" to avoid acknowledging obvious negative situations.
    
39. **Outcome Bias:** The tendency to judge the quality of a decision based purely on its eventual outcome, rather than the logic available at the time it was made.
    
40. **Overconfidence Effect:** Excessive certainty in one's own answers, estimates, or abilities.
    
41. **Pessimism Bias:** The tendency to overestimate the likelihood of negative things happening, particularly when in a depressed or anxious state.
    
42. **Placebo Effect:** The tendency to experience physical or psychological improvements solely due to the belief that a fake or inert intervention will work.
    
43. **Planning Fallacy:** The persistent tendency to underestimate how much time, money, or effort a future task will take to complete.
    
44. **Pro-Innovation Bias:** The tendency to have excessive optimism towards a new invention's usefulness while failing to identify its limitations.
    
45. **Projection Bias:** The tendency to mistakenly assume that your future self will share your exact same current preferences, thoughts, and values.
    
46. **Recency Effect:** The tendency to weigh recent events or recently learned information much more heavily than earlier data.
    
47. **Rhyme as Reason Effect:** The cognitive shortcut where rhyming statements or slogans are perceived as being more truthful or accurate.
    
48. **Risk Compensation (Pelzman Effect):** The tendency to take much greater risks when your perceived safety increases (e.g., driving faster because you wear a seatbelt).
    
49. **Self-Serving Bias:** The tendency to attribute personal successes to internal factors (skill) and personal failures to external factors (bad luck).
    
50. **Spotlight Effect:** The tendency to drastically overestimate how much other people notice your appearance, mistakes, or behavior.
    
51. **Status Quo Bias:** The irrational preference for the current state of affairs, feeling that any change from the baseline is inherently a loss.
    
52. **Survivorship Bias:** The logical error of concentrating exclusively on the people or things that "survived" a process and overlooking those that failed, because they are no longer visible.
    
53. **Zeigarnik Effect:** The phenomenon where uncompleted, interrupted, or paused tasks are remembered much better than completed ones.
    
54. **Zero-Risk Bias:** The preference for completely eliminating a small risk down to zero, over taking a strategy that would cause a much greater overall reduction in a larger risk.
