# ⚛️ Integral

**🔗 Related:** [[Derivative]], [[Area Under Curve]], [[Accumulation]], [[Summation]], [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> The **Integral** is a machine for **accumulation**. It takes all the tiny, instant changes (derivatives) and adds them up to tell you the _total_ result.

**The Problem it Solves:** Finding the area of a square is easy (Length × Width). But how do you find the area of a blob, a cloud, or a weird curve? The Integral solves this by slicing the weird shape into infinitely thin rectangles, calculating the area of each one, and then adding them all up instantly.

### 📝 Key Insight
* - **The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:**
    
    - This is the bridge that connects the two concepts. It says that **Differentiation** (finding the slope) and **Integration** (finding the area) are opposites, just like multiplication and division.
        
    - If you take a function, differentiate it, and then integrate the result, you get back the original function. You just sliced the apple and then glued it back together.
        
- **Accumulation in Real Life:**
    
    - **Physics:** Acceleration $\to$ Velocity $\to$ Position.
        
    - **Business:** Marginal Revenue (profit from next sale) $\to$ Total Revenue (profit from all sales).
        
    - **Biology:** Growth Rate (cells per day) $\to$ Total Size (size of the tumor).
