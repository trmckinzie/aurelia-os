# ⚛️ Glial Cells

**🔗 Related:** [[Neuron]], [[Myelination]], [[Astrocytes]], [[Microglia]], [[Action Potential]], [[Blood-Brain Barrier]], [[Synaptic Pruning]]

---

### 💡 Definition
> Non-neuronal cells in the central and peripheral nervous systems that do not produce electrical impulses (Action Potentials). They maintain homeostasis, form myelin, and provide support and protection for neurons.

- **The Name:** "Glia" comes from the Greek word for "glue." Early anatomists literally thought these cells were just the biological cement holding the important cells (neurons) together.
    
- **The Ratio:** It was long taught that glia outnumber neurons 10-to-1. Recent, more accurate counting methods (like isotropic fractionation) reveal the ratio in the human brain is actually much closer to **1-to-1** (roughly 86 billion of each).

### 📝 Key Insight
* **The "Einstein Brain" Phenomenon:** * When scientists finally analyzed Albert Einstein's brain, they found it weighed _less_ than average and had an unremarkable number of neurons.

- However, researchers found that his brain had a significantly **higher ratio of glial cells to neurons**, particularly in the left parietal lobe (an area associated with mathematical and spatial reasoning).
    
- **The Paradigm Shift:** This helped prove that glia do not just passively support neurons; they actively participate in learning, memory, and cognitive processing by regulating how synapses form and fire.

### 👷 The Four Main Types (The Support Staff)

If neurons are the "rockstars" performing on stage, glial cells are the roadies, security guards, and sound engineers making the show possible.

1. **Astrocytes (The Caretakers):** Star-shaped and the most abundant. They wrap around synapses and blood vessels. They feed the neurons glucose, mop up excess neurotransmitters after a signal fires, and construct the **Blood-Brain Barrier**.
    
2. **Oligodendrocytes (The Insulators - CNS):** As noted in your [[Myelination]] concept, these cells reach out with multiple "arms" to wrap layers of fat (myelin) around the axons of neurons in the brain and spinal cord, speeding up electrical transmission.
    
3. **Schwann Cells (The Insulators - PNS):** These do the exact same job as Oligodendrocytes, but for the rest of the body (Peripheral Nervous System). Instead of reaching out with arms, the _entire cell_ rolls itself around the axon like a pig in a blanket.
    
4. **Microglia (The Immune System):** The brain is isolated from the body's normal immune system. Microglia are the brain's dedicated, specialized white blood cells. They roam around looking for damaged cells, infectious agents, or plaques, and literally eat them (phagocytosis).
