# 👤 James Clear

### 📝 Profile & Context
> A writer on habit formation whose contribution is less theoretical than architectural. Clear's argument is that behavior change fails when it is treated as a motivation problem, and succeeds when it is treated as a **design** problem: motivation is unreliable and expensive, so the durable move is to alter the environment and the cue structure until the desired behavior is the path of least resistance. He is essentially applying an energy-budget argument to daily life without naming it as one.

### 📚 Key Works (In Vault)
- [[Atomic Habits]] (The four-law framework for building and breaking habits).

### ⚛️ Core Concepts
- **[[Habit]]:** The core unit. A behavior automated to the point that it no longer draws on conscious control — which is precisely what makes it cheap.
- **[[The Four Laws of Behavior Change]]:** Make it obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying — and their inversions for breaking a habit. A checklist for cue, craving, response, and reward.
- **[[Identity Based Habits]]:** The claim that outcome-based goals ("run a marathon") are weaker than identity-based ones ("be a runner"), because identity supplies the default rather than requiring a decision.
- **[[The 1% Rule]]:** Compounding applied to behavior — the observation that the daily increment is invisible and the aggregate is not.
- **[[System 1 vs System 2 (Dual-Process Theory)|System 1]]:** Where the whole project is aimed. Every technique in the book is a method for moving a behavior out of effortful control and into automaticity.
