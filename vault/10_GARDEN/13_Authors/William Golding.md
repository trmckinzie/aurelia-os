# 👤 William Golding

### 📝 Profile & Context
> An English novelist and schoolmaster who won the Nobel Prize in Literature (1983). Golding served in the Royal Navy through the Second World War, and his fiction is a sustained argument against the view that cruelty is something civilization installs in people rather than something it restrains. His subject is the thinness of the social layer — not that humans are evil, but that order is an active, maintained achievement, and that its collapse is faster and more ordinary than we prefer to believe.

### 📚 Key Works (In Vault)
- [[Lord of the Flies]] (Schoolboys on an island, and how quickly the rules stop holding).

### ⚛️ Core Concepts
- **[[Intergroup Bias]]:** The novel's central mechanism. The split into hunters and the rest is not caused by scarcity or ideology; the coalition forms first and the justification arrives afterward.
- **[[Aggression]]:** Golding is interested in aggression as something *licensed* by group membership rather than expressed in spite of it — the mask and the chant do the work.
- **[[Social Hierarchy]]:** The book is a study of a dominance contest with no institution left to adjudicate it, which is roughly the condition Sapolsky's unstable-hierarchy findings describe.
- **[[Morality]]:** Read alongside [[Jonathan Haidt]], the island reads less as a fall from innocence than as intuitions running without the institutional scaffolding that normally channels them.
