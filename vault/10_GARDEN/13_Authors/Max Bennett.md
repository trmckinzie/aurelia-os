# 👤 Max Bennett

### 📝 Profile & Context
> An AI entrepreneur and writer who came to neuroscience from the engineering side rather than the lab. His argument is that the history of intelligence is not a smooth ramp from simple to complex, but a series of distinct **breakthroughs** — each one a new computational trick that unlocked a whole class of behavior, layered on top of the machinery that came before rather than replacing it. He reads brain evolution the way an engineer reads a legacy codebase: nothing was designed, everything was patched, and the old layers are still running in production.

### 📚 Key Works (In Vault)
- [[A Brief History of Intelligence]] (Five breakthroughs, from steering to speaking).

### ⚛️ Core Concepts
- **[[Evolutionary Brain Stratification]]:** The organizing claim of his work — the brain is an accretion of layers, each solving a problem the previous layer could not, none of them ever removed.
- **[[Reinforcement Learning]]:** Bennett treats the earliest vertebrate breakthrough as essentially a biological implementation of temporal-difference learning, which makes **[[Dopamine (Reward Prediction Error)|dopamine]]** an error signal rather than a pleasure signal.
- **[[Simulation]]:** The mammalian breakthrough — the ability to run a model of the world offline and evaluate an action before taking it, rather than learning only from consequences already suffered.
- **[[Theory of Mind]]:** The primate breakthrough. Modeling *another* agent's model is what makes teaching, deception, and social learning possible.
- **[[Neural Network]]:** Because he writes from AI, the book runs a continuous comparison between what evolution converged on and what deep learning converged on — and is careful about where the analogy stops.
