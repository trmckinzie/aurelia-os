# 👤 George Miller

### 📝 Profile & Context
> **The Architect of the Cognitive Revolution.** George Miller began his career steeped in the dominant behaviorist tradition of the 1940s, studying speech and communication. However, his exposure to Claude Shannon's newly published **Information Theory** changed his trajectory. He realized that humans, much like telephone wires, have a strict "channel capacity"—a limit to how much information they can process at once.

In 1956, he published what is arguably the most famous paper in the history of psychology: _"The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two."_ This paper, presented at the legendary MIT symposium, proved that the mind is not an infinite sponge or a mysterious black box; it is a measurable computational mechanism with strict hardware limitations.

To solidify the revolution, he co-founded the **Center for Cognitive Studies** at Harvard in 1960 alongside Jerome Bruner, officially institutionalizing the cognitive shift and driving the final nail into the coffin of radical behaviorism.

### 📚 Key Works (In Vault)
- [[The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two]] (1956) — _The paper that quantified the limits of short-term/working memory._
    
- [[Plans and the Structure of Behavior]] (1960) — _Co-authored with Eugene Galanter and Karl Pribram, this book applied cybernetics to human psychology._
    
- [[WordNet]] (1990s) — _A massive, machine-readable lexical database of the English language that Miller helped create, which became a foundational tool for modern Natural Language Processing (NLP) and AI._

### ⚛️ Core Concepts
- [[The Magical Number 7±2]] — _The theory that the average human can only hold roughly seven individual items in their working memory at any given moment._ [[Working Memory]]
    
- [[Chunking]] — _Miller's proposed "hack" for the 7±2 limit. By grouping individual pieces of data into meaningful larger units (chunks), the brain can compress information and store vastly more data in the same amount of working memory slots._
    
- [[Channel Capacity]] — _A concept borrowed from computer science and radio engineering, applied to the human nervous system to describe our biological bandwidth limit for processing sensory input._
    
- [[TOTE Unit (Test-Operate-Test-Exit)]] — _A cybernetic model introduced to replace the overly simplistic behaviorist "Stimulus-Response" arc. It models human behavior as a continuous feedback loop, much like a thermostat._
