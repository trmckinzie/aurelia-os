# ⚙️ 06_AUTODIDACTIC_LEARNING

## 📋 The Sequence
- [ ] **Trigger:** Enter a dedicated Deep Work environment to minimize attention residue. Identify one "Mental Gestalt" or core concept to master.
- [ ] **Phase 1: Metacognitive Mapping (5m):** Perform a "Gap Analysis" by assessing prior knowledge and identifying specific internal assets and deficiencies.
- [ ] **Phase 2: Intentional Acquisition (15-25m):** Consume material while applying "The Franklin Method". Take brief "sentiment hints" rather than verbatim notes to focus on causal schemes and underlying principles.
- [ ] **Phase 3: Effortful Retrieval (10-20m):** Close all sources. Perform "Free Recall" by reconstructing the logic or proofs from scratch to build storage strength. 
- [ ] **Phase 4: Stepwise Correction (5-10m):** Compare your reconstruction to the original "mentor text". Use "retrospective monitoring" to identify and calibrate "illusions of knowing".

## 🧠 System Logic
> This protocol leverages **"Desirable Difficulties"**—tasks that may slow short-term performance but maximize long-term retention. By prioritizing **Retrieval Practice** over passive review, the learner strengthens neural circuits through **myelination**, effectively turning effortful cognitive rules into intuitive expert performance. The sequence is designed to maintain high **Storage Strength** by forcing the brain to access knowledge from scratch, ensuring the cognitive architecture remains resilient across varied contexts.



## 📦 Assets
> - **Environmental Design:** Digital blocking (e.g., Cold Turkey) and physical "Do Not Disturb" rituals to protect focus.
> - **The CHC Model:** A framework for organizing knowledge (Gc) around causal schemes rather than superficial features.
> - **Stepwise Cueing:** A hierarchy of prompts (Free Recall → Partial Hints → Targeted Review) to target specific memory strengths.
> - **The Spectator Method:** Benjamin Franklin's precursor to deliberate practice involving delayed reconstruction and comparison.
