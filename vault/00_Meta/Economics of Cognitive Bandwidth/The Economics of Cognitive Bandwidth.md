
# 🌌 The Economics of Cognitive Bandwidth

> **The Vault Manifesto:** This vault is not a dictionary, an encyclopedia, or a passive collection of book summaries. It is a dynamic, interdisciplinary **Nomological Network**. Its purpose is to map the structural and functional reality of the human mind—from the subatomic firing of a neuron to the subjective realization of spiritual enlightenment.

---

## 🏛️ The Core Thesis

The overarching framework governing every note in this vault is **The Economics of Cognitive Bandwidth**.

The human brain is the most complex computational engine in the known universe, but it operates under severe metabolic and physical constraints. It consumes 20% of the body's energy while making up only 2% of its mass. Because true, high-resolution computation (**[[System 2]]**) is so expensive, evolution designed the brain to aggressively compress data, take shortcuts, and generate illusions to ensure survival.

Every human behavior, cognitive bias, emotional trigger, and spiritual friction point (**[[Dukkha]]**) can be reverse-engineered by understanding how the brain manages this limited bandwidth.

## 🏗️ The Vault Architecture (The Three Stacks)

To prevent academic silos, this vault is organized "Core-Outward." We track the flow of information from the physical hardware up to the conscious experience. Every concept must eventually map to one of these three stacks:

### 1. The Wetware Stack (Hardware & Evolution)

- **The Scope:** The physical, biological foundation. How evolutionary pressures shaped the anatomy of the nervous and endocrine systems.
    
- **Core Disciplines:** [[Evolutionary Biology]], [[Cellular Neuroscience]], [[Behavioral Endocrinology]].
    
- **Key Thinkers:** [[Robert Sapolsky]], [[Leda Cosmides]], [[John Tooby]].
    
- **The Question:** _What is the machine built to do, and how does the wiring work?_
    

### 2. The Software Stack (Information Processing)

- **The Scope:** The algorithms, heuristics, and predictive models running on the wetware. How the brain processes information, calculates risk, and makes decisions under load.
    
- **Core Disciplines:** [[Cognitive Psychology]], [[Behavioral Economics]], [[Systems Theory]].
    
- **Key Thinkers:** [[Daniel Kahneman]], [[Amos Tversky]], [[John Sweller]].
    
- **The Question:** _How does the machine compress data to save energy, and where does the code glitch?_
    

### 3. The User Interface (UI) Stack (Subjective Experience)

- **The Scope:** The first-person, phenomenological experience of being the software running on the wetware. How contemplative practices can debug the interface and reduce psychological suffering.
    
- **Core Disciplines:** [[Secular Buddhism]], [[Philosophy of Mind]], [[Phenomenology]].
    
- **Key Thinkers:** [[Sam Harris]], [[Joseph Goldstein]], [[Anil Seth]].
    
- **The Question:** _What does it feel like to be the machine, and how do we hack the UI (the Ego) to see reality clearly?_

## ⚖️ The Rules of the Core-Outward Curriculum

To ensure the vault grows sustainably and indefinitely without succumbing to "Scope Creep," I adhere to the following operational rules:

1. **Stack Rotation:** I study in monthly cycles, rotating through Wetware (Week 1), Software (Week 2), UI (Week 3), and Synthesis (Week 4). This guarantees a holistic, balanced network.
    
2. **Quality over Completion:** In my 30-minute blocks, the goal is not to finish the book; the goal is to extract 1-3 profound, high-yield `[[Concepts]]`.
    
3. **Just-In-Time Generation:** I do not bulk-generate notes. I leave empty `[[Ghost Links]]` for adjacent concepts. I only run `/t-concept` on a note when it is the explicit focus of my daily reading or an absolute prerequisite for understanding.
    
4. **The Nomological Bridge:** The most important rule of the vault. During "Synthesis Week," I must physically connect the stacks. An isolated Wetware concept is useless unless I can use the `🔗 Related: [[ ]]` field to bridge it to its Software outcome or UI experience.
